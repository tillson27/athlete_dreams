# Trademark Clearance & Filing Brief — "ATHLETE ARC"

> **Purpose:** Hand-off brief for a Canadian trademark agent (also usable for a parallel US filing) to run a formal clearance search and prepare applications for the "ATHLETE ARC" brand.
>
> **Status:** Draft for review. Based on a preliminary knockout screen only — **not legal advice or a formal clearance.** Figures and conflicts below reflect public-source research as of July 2026 and must be verified by the agent.
>
> **Visual identity locked 2026-07-25.** This brief now covers **two** searches and **two** filings — the word mark *and* the figurative mark. See §5.

## 1. Mark

- **Primary mark:** `ATHLETE ARC` — word mark, standard characters (no logo/color claim), to keep protection broad and portable across a future logo.
- **Do NOT file bare `ARC`** — it is heavily diluted (Synopsys, Intel, et al. across many classes) and abuts Arc'teryx; a standalone "ARC" application would be weak and invite conflict. Anchor the distinctive rights in the two-word mark.
- **Stylized logo mark — now actionable, no longer optional-future.** The visual identity was locked on 2026-07-25, satisfying the condition previously set here. Separate application from the word mark. Scope and search requirements in §5.

## 2. Jurisdictions & filing basis

- **Canada (CIPO)** — primary/home market. Canada uses Nice classification and requires goods/services in **specific, ordinary commercial terms** (no class headings alone).
- **United States (USPTO)** — file on **§1(b) intent-to-use** if not yet in commerce; note the US requires narrower, more specific IDs than Canada and eventual proof of use per class.
- Ask the agent whether a **Madrid Protocol** international application (Canada as base) is cheaper than separate national filings for target countries.

## 3. Classes & goods/services

Tiered: file **Core** now; **Optional** as budget/roadmap allow.

### Class 42 — Software / SaaS platform · **CORE**
*The profile + platform engine.*

> Software as a service (SaaS) featuring software for creating, hosting and managing athlete profiles and personal athlete web pages; providing temporary use of online non-downloadable software for building athlete profiles, sharing athletic stories, results and updates, and connecting athletes with supporters and followers; hosting an online platform for athletes to showcase accomplishments, events and training.

### Class 36 — Crowdfunding / fundraising · **CORE**
*Pillar 1 — primary go-to-market.*

> Crowdfunding services; charitable fundraising services, namely, providing an online platform for supporters to financially back athletes for specified events, travel, equipment and training; financial sponsorship matchmaking; providing an online marketplace for the collection and distribution of financial contributions to athletes.

### Class 9 — Downloadable app · **CORE (file if a mobile app is on the roadmap)**
*Covers a future iOS/Android app; distinct from the Class 42 web SaaS.*

> Downloadable mobile application software for creating and managing athlete profiles, discovering and following athletes, and making financial contributions to support athletes.

### Class 35 — Sponsorship discovery, advertising, ambassador programs · **CORE for pillars 2 & 3**
*Pillars 2 (brand↔athlete sponsorship) and 3 (managed ambassador programs).*

> Promoting the goods and services of others through sponsorship arrangements with athletes; advertising and marketing services connecting brands with athletes for sponsorship and ambassador opportunities; providing an online searchable directory of athletes for sponsorship and endorsement purposes; business administration of athlete ambassador and brand-ambassador programs on behalf of others; business networking services in the field of sports.

### Class 41 — Sports content, community & information · **OPTIONAL (recommended)**
*Athlete stories/content, community, sports info — strengthens the "story + community" differentiator.*

> Providing a website featuring non-downloadable content in the nature of athlete stories, athletic results, training information and event updates; providing online sports information; entertainment services, namely, providing an online community for athletes and their supporters to share athletic content; organizing and promoting sporting events for others.

### Class 45 — Online social networking · **OPTIONAL**
*The follow/community layer, if the networking function should be protected in its own right.*

> Internet-based social networking services; providing an online community platform for registered users to form groups, follow athletes, and interact around athletic activities.

## 4. Explicit exclusions

- **Do NOT file in Class 25 (clothing/apparel/footwear)** or Class 28 (sporting goods/equipment). This is a deliberate guardrail: **Class 25 is exactly where Arc'teryx is strongest**, and staying out of it is the cleanest coexistence argument. Do not sell branded apparel under "ATHLETE ARC" without separate legal review.

## 5. Figurative mark — description, search & filing

### 5.1 The mark

A wide letter **"A"** with a flat apex and open (crossbar-less) counter, crossed by a **tapered orange crescent arc** that extends past both legs. Used above or beside the `ATHLETE ARC` wordmark set in a bold geometric sans.

- **Artwork of record:** `client/lib/brand.ts` — single source of truth for the paths and colours. Rendered lockups ship from `client/components/site/Logo.tsx`.
- **Colours:** ink `#181c1e`, arc `#ff5f1f`, ground `#f7fafc`. Advise whether to claim colour or file without a colour claim; a mark filed in black and white is generally broader.

### 5.2 Design-code search — required, and distinct from the word search

**A word-mark search cannot surface figurative conflicts.** The registers index logos by the *elements they depict*, not by the owner's brand name, so a visually confusable mark owned by a differently-named company will not appear in any `ATHLETE ARC` search. Please run a figurative search in addition to the word search:

- **Canada (CIPO)** and **TMview** by **Vienna Classification**; **USPTO** by **design search code**. Assign the operative codes yourself — do not rely on this document. The elements to be classified are: a **letter of the alphabet**, a **triangular / geometric form**, and a **curved band or arc**.
- **Expect heavy noise.** "Bold letter plus swoosh" is a saturated construction in sport and fitness. Please filter for materiality against Classes **42, 36, 35 and 41** rather than returning the raw result set.
- **Freedom to operate is a separate question from registrability.** Even if we decide not to register the figurative mark, *using* it can infringe a registered design mark. We want the search regardless of the filing decision.

### 5.3 Authorship disclosure

The logo was **AI-generated and then redrawn**. Trademark rights arise from use in commerce, so we do not expect this to affect registrability — but **copyright** in purely AI-generated artwork is weak to non-existent in both Canada and the US. Please advise whether a further human redraw is warranted to establish a copyright interest alongside the trademark, and whether this needs disclosing on either application.

## 6. Prior-rights watchlist

Ask the agent to specifically assess these against the classes above.

1. **Arc'teryx / Amer Sports Canada Inc.** — owns `ARC'TERYX` (not bare "ARC"), Canadian, aggressive enforcer, apparel/retail (Class 25/35). Assess likelihood-of-confusion exposure given the shared "ARC" element **and their home-market status in Canada.** **Now also a figurative question:** their mark is a dark, angular, roughly triangular form, and ours is a dark triangular letterform. Assess whether the verbal and visual similarities compound into a single overall commercial impression.
2. **Athleta / Gap Inc.** — **added 2026-07-25, not previously screened.** `ATHLETA` is one letter from `ATHLETE`, operates across North America in the athletic space, and uses a stylized letter-based mark. Assess both the verbal proximity of `ATHLETA` to `ATHLETE ARC` and any figurative overlap with our "A" mark. Exposure is likely concentrated in Class 35 retail rather than our core 42/36, but confirm.
3. **arc.link / "Powered by ARC" (formerly Athelink)** — likely **unregistered/common-law** user in the same niche (athlete profile builder), operating since ~2014, ~11–50 employees, corporate entity obscured (privacy-shielded WHOIS, no entity named on LinkedIn). **Determine whether they hold any Canadian or US registration and whether they have Canadian market presence** — common-law rights only reach where a party actually operates.
4. **Diluted bare-"ARC" registrations** (Synopsys – semiconductors; Intel – GPUs; others) — confirm none sit in Classes 36/42/35/41 in a way that blocks the mark.
5. **Unnamed figurative holders** — by definition these cannot be listed here, since a name-based screen cannot find them. This is the gap §5.2 exists to close.

## 7. Questions for the agent

1. Given the "ARC" crowding, is `ATHLETE ARC` registrable as-is, or should a more distinctive/coined element be added to strengthen it?
2. Does a **coexistence agreement** with arc.link become advisable if the search shows real overlap — or is class separation + the "Athlete" prefix sufficient?
3. Given how saturated the "bold letter + swoosh" form is, is the figurative mark **distinctive enough to be worth registering at all**, or is the budget better spent entirely on the word mark? We would rather hear this than file a weak design mark.
4. **Sequencing and budget:** if both filings cannot be funded at once, confirm the word mark takes priority — our reasoning is that a name is far costlier to change than a logo. Say so if you disagree.
5. Does the figurative mark change your view on the Class 25 exclusion in §4, or is staying out of apparel still the cleanest coexistence argument with Arc'teryx?
